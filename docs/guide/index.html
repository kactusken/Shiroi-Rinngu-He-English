The Game

Shiroi Ringu e (白いリングへ, "To the White Ring"), subtitled Twinkle Little Star Story, is a women's wrestling training game published by Pony Canyon for the Super Famicom in 1995. It was released only in Japan.

You are a new trainee at LLPW, a real Tokyo promotion of the time, and you arrive at its dojo on April 1, 1994. Six years later, on March 31, 2000, you face Shinobu Kandori, "the strongest", in the main event at the Tokyo Dome. Between the two lies a story told in short scenes with real wrestlers of LLPW, FMW, the JWWF and Michinoku Pro, and a training routine you plan one month at a time.

  • You train, the game wrestles. Each month you pick a senior wrestler to train with for each week and choose how to spend each day off. Matches play out by themselves: what you trained decides how your wrestler fights. See How a Month Works and Matches.
  • Three routes, four endings. One choice in April 1995 makes you Carol Midori's or Kurenai Yasha's attendant, and the Kurenai side can lead on to FMW. The story branches only on your choices and on whether you win certain matches, never on your stats. See The Story in Brief and the Story Guide.
  • Hidden numbers. The game shows few of the numbers that matter. Your training stops when Stress passes a limit the game never shows; this guide explains it in Stress and Focus.
  • A versus mode for two players or for watching, with wrestlers you register after an ending. See VS Mode.

Saving

The game keeps one story save on the cartridge. You can save only from a month's main menu, before you start the month:

  1. On the main menu (Advice / Schedule / Status / Complete), press Start.
  2. A man in a white suit greets you: "We meet again. What brings you here this time?" Choose Save.
  3. "Save anyway?" Yes writes over the save and returns you to the menu.

The game never offers to save at the end of a month, so save at the start of every month. To carry on later, choose Continue on the title menu: it shows your name and the saved date, then asks "Load it?". The same Start menu also offers Load.

The versus mode keeps a separate bank of registered wrestlers in the same battery memory (see VS Mode).

Your First Month

The first year is training only: you turn pro in March 1995 and your first match is on July 24, 1995. What you do in the first weeks sets how fast you can train for the next six years, so it is worth getting right.

Power-on to the title

An opening narration plays over scenes with the cast: "A little star is shining. It climbs high, ever higher, pushing away anyone who tries to catch it." Press A to skip it. After the Pony Canyon logo the title logo animates in; give it a moment, then press Start or A. The title menu offers Continue, New Game and VS. Choose New Game.

Making your wrestler

The profile screen has four fields, filled in order: Name, Sign, Blood and Club History, with a club for each of nine school years (Gr.1 to Gr.6 of elementary school, JH1 to JH3 of junior high).

  • Name (up to 8 letters): the keyboard opens on the hiragana page. Press Up to reach the tab row and pick the A tab for Latin letters. A types, B deletes. To finish, pick the fifth tab, the green icon at the end of the tab row (Start does not finish the name).
  • Sign: pick with Up/Down. It adds a few wrestler-type points and matters very little; any sign is fine.
  • Blood: A, B, O or AB. It has no effect on anything.
  • Club History: the one choice here that matters. Every club adds a little to your starting body, and the nine years add up. Your starting HP sets how much you can train, and HP comes mostly from clubs.
Recommended

Soccer Club for all nine years. It is the 33rd club in the list: press Down 32 times from Go-Home Club, then A. You start with HP and Stamina 22 and Strength 19, against 10 and 10 with no club. Swim Club (35th) gives more Strength: six years of Soccer and three of Swim give HP 21 and Strength 22. Every club and what it gives is in Character Creation.

After JH3, choose OK. The story starts.

April 1994

On Friday, April 1, President Rumi Kazama welcomes the six new trainees, and the coach, Harley Saito, explains how training works: "Pro wrestling isn't something you get taught. You watch, you learn, you learn it with your body, and you steal the technique." Then the main menu appears.

  1. Schedule: the month appears week by week. For each week, press A, pick a partner, and press Down for the next week. Put Carol Midori on all five weeks: she costs 3 Stamina a day, the least of anyone, and gives the most HP per Stamina. Harley Saito (8 a day) and Michiko Omukai (10) are more than 22 Stamina can carry.
  2. Press Start and save.
  3. Complete, then OK: the month plays. Training days run by themselves (press A to hurry the animations), and after every Saturday the status window opens so you can check Stamina and Stress.
  4. On Sundays and holidays you choose a day-off place. In the first year there are four: Dining, Movies, Running and Full Rest. Choose by the rule below.
  5. On April 30 the Results window shows how each stat changed this month. One of the trainees runs away in the night, and May's menu comes up.

The Stress trap

Training raises Stress, and once Stress is higher than Focus your wrestler refuses to train: every training day is lost. Focus starts at only 10 and the game never shows it. Full Rest refills Stamina but does nothing for Stress, so resting every Sunday locks you out of training by May. Choose each day off like this instead:

Off: where to go

  1. Stress within 3 of your Focus, or above it → Dining (Stress −10).
  2. Otherwise, Stamina below about 60% of HP → Full Rest (Stamina + half your HP).
  3. Otherwise → Movies (Stress −5, and Focus +2 for good).

Take Movies on the first few days off while Stamina is still full, to raise Focus early. Keep a running count of Focus yourself: 10, plus 2 for each Movies, plus 3 for each match later on (see Stress and Focus).

From May or June, once HP is past 20-25, start mixing in Harley Saito and Miki Handa, with a week of Carol whenever Stamina runs short. The Strategy section has the plan for the rest of the game.

Controls

Everything in this game is a menu or a text box: the D-pad moves, A chooses, B goes back. You never control your wrestler: matches play themselves. That covers almost everything; the rest of this section is what the game doesn't tell you.

What the game doesn't tell you

Saving
Start on a month's main menu is the only way to save. There is no save prompt anywhere else (see Saving).
The opening
A skips the narration; Start doesn't. On the title, wait for the logo to settle before pressing anything.
Text
Hold A to make text type faster. Scenes can't be skipped.
Your name
The top row of the keyboard switches between hiragana, katakana, Latin letters (A) and kanji. To finish, pick the fifth tab, the green icon at the end of that row: Start does nothing here.
Schedule
The cursor moves a whole week at a time, and one pick fills every training day of that week.
Status
The four pages turn with Up and Down, not Left and Right.
Training days
Press A to cut the exercise animations short.
Days off
You have to pick a place: B doesn't back out. The list opens on the place you chose last time.
Matches
No input at all, and Start doesn't pause.

How a Month Works

Every month follows the same pattern: plan it on the main menu, then watch it play out day by day, with story scenes on fixed dates and, from July 1995, matches.

The main menu

The menu shows its four items in capitals; this guide writes them as Advice, Schedule, Status and Complete.

A training day

On each training day the game does four things:

  1. Can she train at all? The day is lost if Stamina is lower than the partner's daily cost, if Stress is higher than Focus, or if a body part is badly injured. A lost day only adds 1 Stress (the results say just "Stress up by 1."), and makes the next day's training a little more likely to succeed.
  2. The partner's daily bonus is added: mostly wrestler-type and technique points.
  3. The exercises. The partner's exercises are tried in her order, each with a chance of success: 80% (90% for a wrestler who had no club) while the exercise's main stat is under 40, then falling: 50% at 100, 10% at 180. Every lost day in a row adds 5%, and every 50 Stress takes 10% away. Once one exercise succeeds, it and every later exercise in her list count. If none succeeds, Stress +1.
  4. Stamina goes down by the partner's daily cost (listed in Training Partners).

End of the week: if Stamina is below half of HP, a hidden weight value drops by 10. Training almost always ends a week below half HP, so this value stays near 0; nothing you do will change that much.

End of the month: the Results window shows the month's change in each stat. Height and Weight are worked out again.

Stress and Focus

Stress is on Status page 2. It goes up with some exercises and with every lost day. Focus is the limit: while Stress is higher than Focus, she will not train. The game never shows Focus, so keep count:

  • It starts at 10, plus a little from some clubs (Archery +3; Movie Club, Calligraphy and Tea Ceremony +2).
  • It goes up for good with Movies (+2), Running (+1), Shopping (+5, from 1997) and every match (+3).

Stress comes down only on days off: Karaoke Box −20, Dining and Shopping −10, Movies −5. In the first two years Dining and Movies are all you have, so take Movies early and often to raise the limit.

Stats

The story never looks at a stat. Stats matter because they win matches, and match results steer the story and decide the ending. Several stats on the status screen do nothing at all.

HP and Strength are averages. Your body has six parts, right and left arm, right and left leg, head and body, and each has its own HP and Strength. Training and clubs raise the parts; Status shows the averages. In a match, damage comes off the part a move hits.

Moves are learned by stats alone. Before every match the game gives your wrestler every move whose stat requirements she meets. No event teaches a move; raising Pro Wrestling and the technique stats does.

Matches

From July 1995 the story books matches on fixed dates: 52 to 65 in all, depending on the route. You only watch. There are no commands: both wrestlers fight by themselves, and the result screen shows the match time, the winner and the finishing move.

How a match runs

  1. The first attack depends on the two wrestlers' styles. Your style is Strong or Heel (see Stats).
  2. The attacker keeps attacking until she is countered. Turns don't alternate: the roles swap only when the defender counters. In a tag match the side that has just taken over may tag in her partner.
  3. Choosing a move. If the defender is caught in a hold, the attacker keeps the hold on. If the defender is down with no head or body HP left, the attacker goes for a pin. Otherwise she picks at random from the moves she can use in the current position, never repeating the move that just landed.
  4. Calm. Most moves cost the attacker some Calm, whether they land or not. While the attacker's Calm is higher than the defender's, her landed moves can't be countered.
  5. Hit and counter. The hit chance is the move's base chance plus the attacker's hit stats minus the defender's dodging stats, at most 99%. On a miss the defender counters if she knows a counter to that move. On a hit she may still counter, at about a quarter of the counter's own chance. A miss that can't be countered still lands: a low hit chance doesn't make a move fail, it makes a counter likely.
  6. Damage comes off the HP of the body parts the move targets, mostly head and body; holds also hit arms and legs. A countered move hurts the attacker instead, and the counterer takes over the attack.

How a match ends

Only by pinfall or submission. There is no knockout, count-out, time limit or draw.

  • Pin. About 80 moves leave the opponent pinnable: suplexes, drivers, many top-rope moves, and the pin moves themselves. The referee counts. A wrestler with Calm left and head and body HP above 0 kicks out at once. With head or body HP at 0 or below she recovers slowly during the count and kicks out once both are above 0; she survives a deficit of up to about a quarter of her full HP. With no Calm left, her Calm recovers instead and she kicks out as soon as it is back. Three ends the match.
  • Submission. When a hold leaves its target part at 0 HP or below, the victim gives up unless she passes a Guts check, (Guts ÷ 2 + 1) in 150, at most 85%. A hold repeats every turn until it is countered, so each turn brings a new check. In a tag match the partner may break it up.

The computer wrestlers

  • They never get stronger. Each wrestler's record is fixed and used unchanged in every match, from your debut to the final. Your wrestler is the only one who improves.
  • They start with more Calm. Your Calm is always 100; theirs is 160 to 250. Until theirs falls below yours, their landed moves can't be countered and yours can.
  • Their best moves come late. Each move in a computer wrestler's list has a Calm threshold: she can use it only once her Calm has fallen that far, so finishers come at the end. You, on the other hand, use your whole move list from the first turn.
  • Their full stats and move lists are in Wrestlers.

Strategy

What to aim for

The ending depends on your route and on winning the final on March 31, 2000; on the Carol route you must also beat Kotori Kagono in September 1999. Matches are won with Pro Wrestling first, then head and body HP, Strength and Suplexes (the throws that set up pins), then Flex and Speed (each miss by the opponent is a counter for you). Pro Wrestling unlocks most moves (requirements are often around 100, up to 250) and helps both attack and defence; a full training plan reaches the maximum, 255, around your debut. Strength unlocks the strongest moves.

For the final against Shinobu Kandori, add Guts and arm, leg and head HP: her moves are mostly holds on the right arm, right leg and head (armbars, heel holds, sleepers), and her one pin move is a late Tiger Driver. Ignore Figure, Height, Weight, Singing, Acting and the wrestler types.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Full Rest every Sunday. Stress climbs past Focus and training stops (see The Stress Trap).
  • Partners you can't afford. A partner who costs more than your Stamina wastes the day. Check Advice: each wrestler says how her training compares with your Stamina.
  • Go-Home Club. No club means starting with HP 10 and training at half the pace for months.
  • The Running day off. It costs 20 Stamina for almost nothing.
  • Expecting a stat check. No event looks at your stats. Training for Fame, Figure or a wrestler type gains you nothing.
  • Not saving. One save, only at the start of a month: save every month.

Character Creation

Almost every stat starts at 10. Each school year's club adds its bonuses, the sign adds wrestler-type points, Stress is set to 0, and Stamina is filled to HP. A stat pushed below 0 by a club becomes 0. Clubs' Calm bonuses are lost: the first day of the story sets Calm to 100.

Your year

Six trainees arrive on April 1, 1994: you, Yuka Shimamura, Kotori Kagono from Osaka, Nagi Akizuki, Naomi Iwashita and Kayo Nakamura. Three of them quit in the first months; Kagono leaves for the JWWF; only you and Shimamura pass the pro test in March 1995. All of them come back at the end.

The Story in Brief

The story is a calendar of scenes and matches on fixed dates. It branches in only two ways: your answer at four choices, and whether you win certain matches. No stat is ever checked, so stats change the story only by winning matches. The full walkthrough, month by month for every route, is the Story Guide.

VS Mode

VS on the title menu is the versus mode: Singles Match and Tag Match between wrestlers from your bank, plus Delete Wrestler, Show Password and Enter Password.

  • The bank holds wrestlers you register after finishing the story. On a new cartridge every bank slot is "Empty", and choosing one says "No wrestlers in this bank."
  • Show Password gives a registered wrestler's password; Enter Password adds a wrestler from a password, typed on the same keyboard as the name, so you can trade wrestlers with friends.

Cut from the story

The cartridge holds a second copy of part of the FMW route that nothing plays. In it, Onita sends you and Combat Toyoda to the JWWF in July 1998, where Toyoda confronts Kandori; in August an interpromotional month against LLPW follows. Nine other finished scenes are never played, among them Eagle Sawai winning the Triple Crown in August 1999. All of them are translated in the patch.

Bugs in the original

  • Losing to Kagono ends the Carol route. In the original game, losing the September 14, 1999 match against Kotori Kagono on the Carol route replays the scene before the match and books nothing: there is no final and no ending, and the calendar runs on past March 2000. The English patch fixes this: a loss now plays Kagono's own scene for it, and the story goes on to October. In the Japanese game, keep a save from before the match.
  • Partner costs are read one entry off (see Training Partners).
  • The Sambo Club also applies the Aerobics Gym's effects.
  • Your style looks only at Strong and Entertainment; Babyface, Power and Heel are never considered.

Q&A

Is there an English version?

Not from the publisher: the game was only released in Japan. This guide was written alongside an English fan translation, released as a patch for your own copy of the game.

Can I control my wrestler in matches?

No. Matches are automatic for both sides. You decide how she fights through training: see Matches.

Why did my wrestler stop training?

Either Stamina is below her partner's cost or Stress is above her hidden Focus. A results page that says only "Stress up by 1." means a lost day. See The Stress Trap.

How do I learn new moves?

By stats alone: before each match she gets every move whose requirements she meets. Pro Wrestling unlocks the most. The Moves table lists every requirement.

Which route should I pick?

The Carol route is the classic LLPW story with the regular army. The Kurenai route has Eagle's heel army and Yoshiaki Fujiwara; win the Rookie Tournament match on March 18, 1996 and it offers the FMW route, with Onita, Kudo and a summer at Michinoku Pro. The FMW and Michinoku partners are the best in the game. See the Story Guide.

What happens if I lose a match?

Usually nothing: most matches only change the scene that follows, or nothing at all. Those that change the story are in The Story in Brief. Losing the final gives a different epilogue.

Do Fame, Figure or my wrestler type unlock anything?

No. No story event checks a stat. Fame and Figure are never used.

How long is the game?

Six in-game years, 72 months. A month takes about 4 to 10 minutes to play, depending on how many scenes and matches it has.
